## Abstract

Longer mission lifetimes and improved manoeuvrability enabled by electric propulsion on small satellites have introduced 
complex interactions between radio-frequency (RF) systems and Hall-effect thruster plumes. Dense, partially ionized plumes 
perturb the local plasma environment, potentially degrading RF payload performance through antenna detuning, phase 
instability, and altered wave propagation, particularly for low-frequency systems. This study presents a physics-based 
modelling framework to assess plume–RF interactions affecting VHF Automatic Identification System (AIS) antennas. The 
approach integrates plume flow-field modelling, plasma parameter estimation, and cold-plasma electromagnetic simulations 
to quantify changes in plasma frequency, permittivity, and ion density during thruster operation. Flux-based surface 
accumulation models further evaluate contamination effects from neutralized propellant and charge-exchange ions, revealing 
gradual antenna detuning due to dielectric film formation. Results indicate that low-frequency RF systems are significantly 
more susceptible to plume-induced effects than higher-frequency payloads, underscoring the importance of plume–RF 
coupling analysis in early spacecraft design and motivating mitigation strategies such as increased antenna–thruster 
separation, operational constraints, and protective coatings.
